# Management of Osteoarthritis (2013): 50 MCQs

**Source:** Ministry of Health Malaysia, *Clinical Practice Guidelines: Management of Osteoarthritis* (MaHTAS, 2013), including its tables and appendices.
**Format:** 50 single-best-answer questions, 4 options (A-D), MRCP Part 1 style. At least 20 have long multilayer stems that need clinical reasoning. Several stems reproduce CPG tables as text, with the table or appendix cited. Every wrong answer is a near-miss.
**Note:** Every fact is taken from the CPG text. Practice use only, not for real patient care.

## Section 1: Diagnosis and Investigation

### Q1
**Topic:** ACR clinical criteria for hand OA

A 62-year-old woman has 2 years of aching and stiffness in both hands. Examination shows hard bony enlargement of the 2nd and 3rd DIP joints and the 1st carpometacarpal joint bilaterally, with bony enlargement of both 2nd and 3rd PIP joints, and only one mildly swollen MCP joint. RF is negative.

Adapted from CPG Table 1 (ACR 1990 clinical criteria for idiopathic hand OA):

| Criterion | Requirement |
|---|---|
| 1 | Hand pain, aching or stiffness |
| 2 | Hard tissue enlargement of ≥2 of 10 selected joints (2nd, 3rd DIP; 2nd, 3rd PIP; 1st CMC, both hands) |
| 3 | Fewer than 3 swollen MCP joints |
| 4a | Hard tissue enlargement of ≥2 DIP joints |
| 4b | Deformity of ≥2 of the 10 selected joints |

Diagnosis (clinical only): criteria 1, 2, 3 plus 4a or 4b.

**Does she fulfil the criteria, and what is the performance of these criteria?**

A. No; RF positivity is required
B. No; more than 3 swollen MCP joints are required
C. Yes; sensitivity 98% and specificity 92%
D. Yes; sensitivity 92% and specificity 98%

**Answer:** D

**Explanation:** She has hand pain and stiffness (1), hard tissue enlargement of at least 2 of the 10 selected joints (2), fewer than 3 swollen MCP joints (3), and hard tissue enlargement of at least 2 DIP joints (4a), so she fulfils the criteria. The CPG gives sensitivity 92% and specificity 98% for these hand OA criteria. RF positivity is not part of them, and OA features fewer than 3 swollen MCP joints (more would suggest inflammatory arthritis).

**Teaching point:** Hand OA is clinical: pain/stiffness, bony enlargement of at least 2 of 10 selected joints, fewer than 3 swollen MCPs, and DIP enlargement or deformity.

**Reference:** CPG Section 4.2a, Table 1.

### Q2
**Topic:** ACR criteria for hip OA

A 67-year-old man has 8 months of right groin pain on walking. ESR is 14 mm/hr. A standing pelvic radiograph shows femoral and acetabular osteophytes with preserved axial joint space.

Adapted from CPG Table 2 (ACR 1991 criteria, hip OA): hip pain plus at least 2 of 3: (1) ESR <20 mm/hr; (2) femoral and acetabular osteophytes on X-ray; (3) axial joint space narrowing on X-ray. Sensitivity 89%, specificity 91%.

**Does he meet the criteria?**

A. No, as joint space narrowing is also required
B. Yes, but only if an MRI also shows cartilage loss
C. Yes, with hip pain plus ESR <20 mm/hr and osteophytes
D. No, as ESR must be above 20 mm/hr to be diagnostic

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** The hip OA classification needs hip pain plus at least 2 of 3 features. He has hip pain, ESR below 20 mm/hr and osteophytes on both sides of the joint, which is two of three, so he meets the criteria (sensitivity 89%, specificity 91%). Joint space narrowing is one of the options, not a requirement. The criteria use ESR, not RF.

**Teaching point:** Hip OA: hip pain plus any 2 of ESR <20 mm/hr, osteophytes, axial joint space narrowing.

**Reference:** CPG Section 4.2b, Table 2.

### Q3
**Topic:** Clinical-only knee OA criteria (long clinical stem)

A 58-year-old woman presents with 1 year of right knee pain. Morning stiffness lasts 15 minutes. There is crepitus on movement and bony enlargement of the knee, with no palpable warmth. No bloods or radiographs have been done. She asks whether the diagnosis can be made today.

Adapted from CPG Table 3 (ACR 1986 criteria, knee OA, clinical only): knee pain plus at least 3 of 6: age >50, stiffness <30 min, crepitus, bony tenderness, bony enlargement, no palpable warmth. Sensitivity 95% and specificity 69% with 3 of 6; sensitivity 84% and specificity 89% with 4 of 6.

**What is the most appropriate statement?**

A. Yes: 5 of 6 clinical features are present, so a clinical diagnosis is possible
B. No: an MRI is required in patients who are over 50 years old
C. No: an X-ray with osteophytes is needed first to diagnose
D. No: ESR and RF must be normal before the diagnosis is made

**Answer:** A

**Explanation:** Knee pain plus at least 3 of 6 clinical features allows a clinical-only diagnosis, and she has 5 (age over 50, stiffness under 30 minutes, crepitus, bony enlargement, no palpable warmth). The CPG states OA should be diagnosed clinically (Recommendation 1), with radiographs as supportive, and laboratory tests only to exclude other inflammatory joint disease. Requiring 4 of 6 increases specificity from 69% to 89%.

**Teaching point:** Knee OA can be diagnosed clinically: pain plus 3 of 6 features. Radiographs and bloods are optional.

**Reference:** CPG Section 4.2c, Table 3; Recommendation 1.

### Q4
**Topic:** Stiffness duration

A 55-year-old man has pain and stiffness in both knees. His stiffness after rising from a chair lasts 5 minutes, and in the morning about 10 minutes. A colleague suggests that morning stiffness means inflammatory arthritis.

**What is the most accurate statement?**

A. Stiffness occurs only after prolonged rest of several hours and never on rising from a chair
B. Stiffness in OA usually lasts a few minutes and almost always under 30 minutes, unlike inflammatory arthritis
C. Stiffness in OA usually lasts 30-60 minutes and is worst after inactivity, similar to early RA
D. Stiffness in OA lasts over an hour in the morning and improves after exercise, which distinguishes it from inflammatory arthritis

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Stiffness in OA is a sensation of tightening after inactivity (in the morning or on arising from sitting) that usually lasts only a few minutes and almost always less than 30 minutes, in contrast to inflammatory arthritis such as RA where it is prolonged. Pain in OA is insidious, worse with use and relieved by rest, though night pain may occur in severe disease.

**Teaching point:** OA stiffness: minutes, almost always under 30 minutes.

**Reference:** CPG Section 4.1.

### Q5
**Topic:** Synovial fluid analysis (long clinical stem)

A 66-year-old woman with known knee OA has a swollen right knee. Aspiration yields 20 ml of clear yellow, viscous fluid. Fluid analysis is shown.

Adapted from CPG Table 4 (synovial fluid categories):

| Measure | Non-inflammatory | Inflammatory | Septic |
|---|---|---|---|
| Clarity | Transparent | Translucent | Opaque |
| Viscosity | High | Low | Variable |
| WBC per mm3 | 200-2,000 | 2,000-10,000 | >100,000 |
| PMNs | <25% | ≥50% | ≥75% |
| Culture | Negative | Negative | Often positive |

Her fluid has a WBC of 1,200 per mm3 with 15% PMNs, high viscosity and a negative culture.

**How is the fluid classified?**

A. Haemorrhagic, typical of trauma or a bleeding disorder
B. Septic, needing urgent washout and antibiotics
C. Non-inflammatory, typical of OA
D. Inflammatory, typical of early RA

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** A WBC of 200-2,000 per mm3 with fewer than 25% PMNs, transparent, yellow, viscous fluid and negative culture is non-inflammatory, which is typical of OA. Inflammatory fluid has 2,000-10,000 cells with 50% or more PMNs and low viscosity. Septic fluid usually has over 100,000 cells and is opaque. Synovial fluid analysis is seldom required in OA, except to exclude septic, inflammatory or crystal arthropathy.

**Teaching point:** OA synovial fluid is non-inflammatory: WBC 200-2,000, PMN <25%, high viscosity.

**Reference:** CPG Section 5.1, Table 4.

### Q6
**Topic:** Role of laboratory tests

A 60-year-old woman with typical knee OA has an ESR of 22 mm/hr and a normal CRP. Her GP wants to order rheumatoid factor, ANA and a full autoimmune screen "to be sure".

**Which statement is consistent with the CPG?**

A. No test is specific; tests are done only to exclude other disease
B. A positive ANA with raised CRP is needed to confirm OA
C. A raised ESR above 40 mm/hr is needed to confirm OA
D. Synovial fluid analysis is needed in every case

**Answer:** A

**Explanation:** There is no specific laboratory test for OA. ESR and CRP are normal or only mildly raised, and synovial fluid is essentially normal. Laboratory investigations may be done to exclude other inflammatory joint disease, and OA should be diagnosed clinically.

**Teaching point:** OA is a clinical diagnosis. Lab tests only exclude inflammatory or crystal arthritis.

**Reference:** CPG Section 5.1; Recommendation 1.

### Q7
**Topic:** Kellgren-Lawrence grading

A 70-year-old man has a standing knee radiograph showing moderate multiple osteophytes, definite joint space narrowing, some sclerosis and possible deformity of the bone ends.

Adapted from CPG Table 5 (Kellgren-Lawrence grades):

| Grade | Description |
|---|---|
| I | Doubtful narrowing, possible osteophytic lipping |
| II | Definite osteophytes, possible narrowing |
| III | Moderate multiple osteophytes, definite narrowing, some sclerosis, possible deformity |
| IV | Large osteophytes, marked narrowing, severe sclerosis, definite deformity |

**Which grade is this?**

A. Grade I
B. Grade IV
C. Grade III
D. Grade II

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Moderate multiple osteophytes, definite joint space narrowing, some sclerosis and possible deformity define Kellgren-Lawrence grade III. Grade IV requires large osteophytes, marked narrowing, severe sclerosis and definite bony deformity, and grade II shows only definite osteophytes with possible narrowing. When radiography is needed in hip or knee OA, it should be a weight-bearing (standing AP) view.

**Teaching point:** K-L: I doubtful, II definite osteophytes, III definite narrowing with moderate osteophytes, IV large osteophytes with severe sclerosis and deformity.

**Reference:** CPG Section 5.2, Table 5.

### Q8
**Topic:** Hand radiographs: OA or inflammatory arthritis (long clinical stem)

A 58-year-old woman has painful, swollen DIP and PIP joints. Radiographs show central erosions with adjacent osteophyte formation, producing a "gull-wing" appearance in several interphalangeal joints. MCPs and wrists are spared. RF and ACPA are negative, and she has no psoriasis.

Adapted from CPG Table 6 (hand X-ray patterns):

| Condition | Typical radiographic change |
|---|---|
| Osteoarthritis | Focal narrowing, marginal osteophyte, sclerosis, osteochondral bodies |
| Erosive OA | Subchondral erosion |
| Psoriatic arthritis | Proliferative marginal erosion, retained or increased bone density |
| Rheumatoid arthritis | Non-proliferative marginal erosion, osteopenia |

**What is the most likely diagnosis?**

A. Psoriatic arthritis
B. Gout
C. Erosive osteoarthritis
D. Rheumatoid arthritis

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Central (subchondral) erosions with osteophytes in the interphalangeal joints, the gull-wing appearance, and sparing of the MCPs and wrists point to erosive OA. RA causes marginal, non-proliferative erosions with osteopenia and involves MCPs and wrists. Psoriatic arthritis shows proliferative marginal erosions with retained bone density. Seronegativity and no psoriasis support erosive OA.

**Teaching point:** Gull-wing erosions with osteophytes at the DIP and PIP joints suggest erosive OA, not RA or psoriatic arthritis.

**Reference:** CPG Section 5.2, Table 6; Figures 4 and 5.

### Q9
**Topic:** Imaging choices

A 64-year-old woman has typical knee OA. Her GP is considering MRI of the knee and musculoskeletal ultrasound to "confirm" the diagnosis.

**Which statement fits the CPG?**

A. Weight-bearing plain radiographs; MRI and ultrasound are seldom needed
B. Non-weight-bearing radiographs are the standard views for OA
C. Ultrasound is the standard imaging modality for knee OA
D. MRI is the standard imaging, with X-rays only if MRI is unavailable

**Answer:** A

**Explanation:** Plain radiography remains the standard imaging for OA, and should be a weight-bearing standing AP view for hip and knee. MRI and musculoskeletal ultrasound are seldom indicated for diagnosis, though ultrasound may be useful in early hand arthritis (it visualises cartilage) and can show synovial inflammation in symptomatic hand and knee OA.

**Teaching point:** Weight-bearing plain X-ray is the standard. MRI and ultrasound are seldom needed.

**Reference:** CPG Section 5.2.

### Q10
**Topic:** Secondary OA

A 38-year-old man has early OA of both hips and the MCP joints of the index and middle fingers, with bronzed skin and diabetes. A colleague suggests this may be "just primary OA".

**Which cause of secondary OA does the CPG list in this metabolic category?**

A. Haemochromatosis
B. Septic arthritis
C. Leg length inequality
D. Legg-Perthes disease

**Answer:** A

**Explanation:** Metabolic causes of secondary OA include acromegaly, haemochromatosis and chondrocalcinosis. Haemochromatosis typically causes arthropathy of the 2nd and 3rd MCPs, with bronzed skin and diabetes. Legg-Perthes disease and leg length inequality are anatomic causes, and septic arthritis is an inflammatory cause. Young onset or atypical joints should prompt a search for a secondary cause.

**Teaching point:** Secondary OA: metabolic (acromegaly, haemochromatosis, chondrocalcinosis), anatomic, traumatic, inflammatory.

**Reference:** CPG Section 3.

### Q11
**Topic:** Risk factors

A 56-year-old woman with a BMI of 34 kg/m2 asks which of her risk factors for knee OA can be changed.

**Which risk factors does the CPG list as modifiable?**

A. Female sex and Heberden's nodes
B. Family history
C. Advancing age and genetic influence
D. Body mass index and malalignment

**Answer:** D

**Explanation:** Non-modifiable risk factors are advancing age, genetic influence (39-65% for hand and knee OA in women) and Heberden's nodes. Modifiable factors are BMI and malalignment (which contributes to progression of knee OA, though results are mixed on incidence). The lifetime risk of knee OA is higher with knee injury and increased BMI.

**Teaching point:** Modifiable: BMI and malalignment. Non-modifiable: age, genetics, Heberden's nodes.

**Reference:** CPG Section 2.2.

### Q12
**Topic:** Epidemiology in Malaysia

A trainee is asked about local data. In the Malaysian COPCORD study, what proportion of adult Malaysians reported knee pain?

**Which figure is correct?**

A. 25%
B. 2.2%
C. 50%
D. 9.3%

**Answer:** D

**Explanation:** In the Malaysian COPCORD study, 9.3% of adult Malaysians had knee pain, and more than half of those examined had clinical evidence of OA. Hip pain was less common, affecting 2.2%. The CPG notes this is probably an underestimate, because it included only those with pain in the past week.

**Teaching point:** COPCORD Malaysia: knee pain 9.3%, hip pain 2.2%.

**Reference:** CPG Section 2.1c.

## Section 2: Non-pharmacological Management

### Q13
**Topic:** Weight reduction

A 57-year-old woman weighing 98 kg with a BMI of 36 kg/m2 has knee OA. She has 3 months of physiotherapy planned and asks how much weight loss helps.

**Which statement fits the CPG evidence?**

A. Weight loss should wait until pain is controlled with drugs first
B. Each unit lost cuts load per step 4-fold, so emphasise weight loss in knee OA
C. Each unit lost cuts load per step 4-fold, and the effect is proven in hip OA
D. Weight loss helps only when it is more than 20% of body weight

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Obesity is an important modifiable risk factor for development and progression of knee OA. In an RCT, each unit of weight loss reduced the load on the knee per step four-fold during daily activities. Weight reduction reduces pain and improves function (Grade A). There is no evidence on the effect of weight loss in hip OA.

**Teaching point:** Emphasise weight loss in overweight knee OA. Each unit lost reduces knee load per step about 4-fold.

**Reference:** CPG Section 6.1b(i); Recommendation 3.

### Q14
**Topic:** Exercise prescription (long clinical stem)

A 63-year-old woman with moderate knee OA, BMI 32 kg/m2 and hypertension is worried that exercise will "wear out" her joint. She has been inactive for a year. Her physiotherapist suggests an individualised programme.

**Which statement best reflects the CPG?**

A. Strengthening is the only proven exercise, and aerobic exercise is not advised
B. Home exercise without supervision is preferred, with one type clearly best
C. Exercise does not worsen OA, and it should be individualised, supervised and regular
D. Exercise speeds cartilage loss, so it should be limited to short, light walks

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Exercise is effective at reducing pain in hip and knee OA. Regular non-competitive exercise does not exacerbate OA or increase the likelihood of joint replacement. Programmes should be individualised, supervised and done regularly (Grade C), with graded activity, goal setting, feedback and positive reinforcement to improve adherence. One type of exercise (strength, aerobic or mixed) has not been shown to be better than another. Land-based or aquatic exercise may be used for short-term benefit (Grade A).

**Teaching point:** Exercise helps OA and does not harm joints. Individualise it, supervise it and keep it regular.

**Reference:** CPG Section 6.1b(ii), 6.1c; Recommendation 4.

### Q15
**Topic:** Aquatic exercise

A 68-year-old man with knee OA and obesity finds land-based exercise painful. His physiotherapist suggests hydrotherapy.

**What does the CPG say about aquatic exercise?**

A. Short-term pain and function improved, but not walking ability or stiffness
B. Short-term pain, walking ability and stiffness all improved
C. It is contraindicated in hip OA but helpful in knee OA
D. It gave benefit lasting 5 years, with no effect on pain relief

**Answer:** A

**Explanation:** A Cochrane review found aquatic exercise gave short-term benefit for hip and knee OA (pain and function), with no statistically significant effect on walking ability and, in the comparison with land-based exercise, no effect on stiffness. Land-based or aquatic exercise may be used for short-term benefit (Grade A).

**Teaching point:** Aquatic exercise helps pain and function short-term, but not walking ability or stiffness.

**Reference:** CPG Section 6.1c(i); Recommendation 4.

### Q16
**Topic:** TENS and thermotherapy

A 72-year-old woman with chronic, moderate to severe knee OA pain is not a candidate for knee replacement. She asks about TENS and heat or cold packs.

**Which statement matches the CPG?**

A. Therapeutic ultrasound reduces pain and improves function in OA
B. Cold packs are proven to improve function when used alone
C. TENS is first-line for all knee OA, and ultrasound is proven
D. TENS evidence is weak; ACR advises it only when TKA is unsuitable

**Answer:** D

**Explanation:** The CPG notes a lack of supporting evidence for TENS in knee OA from the latest Cochrane review, but cites ACR 2012 recommending it for patients with chronic moderate to severe pain who are unsuitable for TKA. ACR also recommends thermal agents with physiotherapist-supervised exercise. Therapeutic ultrasound was inconclusive for pain and function.

**Teaching point:** TENS has weak evidence. Thermal agents are best combined with supervised exercise. Therapeutic ultrasound is inconclusive.

**Reference:** CPG Section 6.1c.

### Q17
**Topic:** Occupational therapy in hand OA (long clinical stem)

A 59-year-old seamstress has painful hand OA with 1st CMC involvement and difficulty writing and sewing. She has tried paracetamol with partial relief. She is reluctant to try more drugs.

**Which non-drug approach is best supported?**

A. Knee-type bracing adapted for the hand joints
B. Complete hand rest in splints for 3 months
C. Early OT referral for joint protection, home exercises and thumb splints
D. Referral to OT only if surgery is being planned

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Early referral to occupational therapy is advised for pain, difficulty and frustration performing activities (Grade A). Joint protection and home exercises increased grip strength by 25% and global hand function by 65%. Thumb splints help reduce pain and improve function, and splinting plus joint protection programmes significantly decrease pain and stiffness in hand OA. There is no evidence for splinting in knee OA.

**Teaching point:** Hand OA: refer early to OT for joint protection, exercises and thumb splints.

**Reference:** CPG Section 6.1d; Recommendation 5.

### Q18
**Topic:** Walking stick prescription

A 76-year-old woman with right hip OA is offered a walking stick. The OT is setting the height.

**How should the stick be measured and used?**

A. At wrist level when standing, and trained in use
B. At hip level, and used on the same side as the sore hip
C. At chest height when standing, and trained in use
D. Any height, provided written instructions are given

**Answer:** A

**Explanation:** The CPG states that the stick handle height should be at wrist level when the user is standing. The CPG also stresses prescribing an appropriate device with proper training. (Standard practice is to hold the stick on the side opposite the affected joint.) Walking sticks and tap turners are adjuncts to core treatment for those with specific ADL problems.

**Teaching point:** Walking stick handle at wrist level (standing). Train the patient to use it.

### Q19
**Topic:** Footwear and braces

A 65-year-old man with medial knee OA asks about a knee brace and special shoes after seeing an advertisement.

**Which statement fits the CPG?**

A. Offer both knee braces and contoured shoes
B. Offer contoured walking shoes, but do not offer knee braces
C. Offer neither, since orthoses are of no benefit in OA
D. Offer knee braces with Grade A evidence, but not contoured shoes

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Walking shoes with neutral, contoured orthoses reduced pain and stiffness and improved function in knee OA at one year (Grade A), and may be offered in hip OA with weaker evidence (Grade C). Knee braces have not been shown to reduce pain, improve function or quality of life, even though widely used, so they should not be offered (Grade A).

**Teaching point:** Offer contoured walking shoes. Do not offer knee braces.

**Reference:** CPG Section 6.1e; Recommendation 6.

### Q20
**Topic:** Patient education

A 61-year-old woman with newly diagnosed knee OA is anxious that she "will end up in a wheelchair". Her GP wants to give her a prescription only.

**Which element is the CPG's integral part of OA management?**

A. Education for surgical patients only, before and after operation
B. A leaflet only, with no tailoring to the individual's goals
C. Education about the disease and treatment, with ongoing participation
D. Drug treatment alone, since education adds no benefit

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Patient education should be an integral part of OA management (Grade A). It should be tailored and cover diagnosis, nature of the disease, therapeutic options and the importance of continuing participation. Patients who understand their disease cope better and report less pain, and the most important goal is to instil a positive attitude.

**Teaching point:** Education is Grade A and core to OA care. Instil a positive attitude.

**Reference:** CPG Section 6.1a; Recommendation 2.

## Section 3: Pharmacological Management

### Q21
**Topic:** First-line analgesic

A 70-year-old woman with mild to moderate knee OA pain, mild hepatic steatosis (normal ALT) and no alcohol intake starts analgesia. She asks what to try first.

**What does the CPG recommend?**

A. Paracetamol as first-line analgesic in mild to moderate pain
B. An NSAID, as an ibuprofen/paracetamol combination is more effective than ibuprofen alone
C. Tramadol, as it is more effective than paracetamol for mild pain
D. Short-course oral prednisolone as the first-line analgesic for pain in OA

**Answer:** A

**Explanation:** Paracetamol can be used in OA (Grade A) and should be the first-line analgesic in mild to moderate pain (Grade C). It is well tolerated and safe, and extended-release forms are superior to placebo. A combination tablet of ibuprofen and paracetamol confers no additional benefit over ibuprofen alone. Typical dosing is 0.5-1 g every 6-8 hours, to a maximum of 4 g/day, with caution in hepatic impairment and alcohol dependence.

**Teaching point:** Paracetamol first (maximum 4 g/day). Ibuprofen-paracetamol combinations add nothing.

**Reference:** CPG Section 6.2a(i); Recommendation 7; Appendix 8.

### Q22
**Topic:** Tramadol in the elderly (long clinical stem)

A 79-year-old woman with painful knee OA takes paracetamol 1 g four times daily with inadequate relief. She has a history of a single seizure 5 years ago, takes sertraline for depression and has had two falls. Her GP considers tramadol.

**Which statement is the most appropriate caution?**

A. Interacts only with NSAIDs; start 100 mg, maximum 400 mg/day
B. Seizure risk and SSRI interaction; start 50 mg, maximum 300 mg/day in the elderly
C. No seizure risk; start 100 mg, maximum 600 mg/day in the elderly
D. Seizure risk only with high doses; start 50 mg, maximum 600 mg/day

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Tramadol may be used alone or with paracetamol (Grade A), with common effects of dizziness, nausea, vomiting, constipation and drowsiness, so it needs caution in the elderly. Appendix 8 warns of seizure risk (history of seizures, high doses), interaction with antidepressants (SSRIs and SNRIs), and in the elderly advises starting at the lowest dose (50 mg) with a maximum of 300 mg daily (adults 400 mg). She has three red flags: seizure history, an SSRI, and falls.

**Teaching point:** Tramadol: caution with seizures, SSRIs/SNRIs and the elderly (start 50 mg, maximum 300 mg/day).

**Reference:** CPG Section 6.2a(ii); Appendix 8; Recommendation 8.

### Q23
**Topic:** NSAID choice with GI risk (long clinical stem)

A 72-year-old man with knee OA and persistent pain despite paracetamol takes low-dose aspirin for ischaemic heart disease and was admitted with an upper GI bleed from a gastric ulcer 3 years ago. He now needs an NSAID-type drug.

**Which approach best fits the CPG?**

A. Use a non-selective NSAID alone, as a COX-2 inhibitor bleeds more
B. Use any NSAID once aspirin is stopped, with no GI protection
C. Avoid NSAIDs; if essential, use a COX-2 inhibitor with a PPI
D. Use two NSAIDs together at low doses, with a PPI

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** In patients with previous GI complications, NSAIDs or COX-2 inhibitors should be avoided (Grade C), but if indicated a COX-2 inhibitor combined with a PPI may be offered (Grade A). Combination of more than one NSAID/COX-2 inhibitor should never be used. He is also at high GI risk by age over 65 and aspirin use, and has ischaemic heart disease, which needs caution with NSAIDs for CV safety.

**Teaching point:** Previous GI bleed: avoid NSAIDs if possible, else COX-2 inhibitor plus PPI. Never combine NSAIDs.

**Reference:** CPG Section 6.2a(iii); Recommendation 9.

### Q24
**Topic:** GI risk definition

A clinic audits prescribing of NSAIDs alone to OA patients at risk of GI complications.

Adapted from CPG Section 11.2 (high GI risk): age >65 years, or on high-dose NSAIDs, aspirin, corticosteroid or anticoagulant, or previous history of upper GI complications.

**Which patient is at high risk?**

A. A 58-year-old man on ibuprofen only, no history
B. A 35-year-old man on a short course of ibuprofen with no history
C. A 68-year-old woman on low-dose aspirin who needs an NSAID
D. A 62-year-old woman on paracetamol who needs an NSAID, with no other medicines

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** High GI risk is defined as age over 65, use of high-dose NSAIDs, aspirin, corticosteroid or anticoagulant, or a previous upper GI complication. The 68-year-old on aspirin meets two of these. In high-risk patients, COX-2 inhibitors are preferred to non-selective NSAIDs with a PPI for primary ulcer prevention (Grade A). The audit indicator tracks patients at risk who receive an NSAID alone.

**Teaching point:** High GI risk: age >65, high-dose NSAID/aspirin/steroid/anticoagulant, or previous GI complication.

**Reference:** CPG Section 11.2; Recommendation 9.

### Q25
**Topic:** Celecoxib and sulfonamide allergy

A 64-year-old woman with knee OA is prescribed celecoxib 200 mg daily. She reports a previous severe rash to co-trimoxazole (a sulfonamide antibiotic).

**Which statement is the most accurate?**

A. Contraindicated with sulfonamide allergy; OA maximum 100 mg/day
B. Contraindicated with sulfonamide hypersensitivity; OA maximum 200 mg/day
C. Contraindicated only with hypertension; OA maximum 400 mg/day
D. Safe with sulfonamide allergy; OA maximum 400 mg/day

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Appendix 8 lists hypersensitivity to sulfonamides as a contraindication to celecoxib. The recommended daily maximum is 200 mg for OA (400 mg for inflammatory arthritis). Celecoxib also carries cautions for ischaemic heart disease, cerebrovascular disease and renal impairment. Patients allergic to one NSAID may be allergic to others.

**Teaching point:** Celecoxib: contraindicated with sulfonamide allergy. Maximum 200 mg/day in OA.

**Reference:** CPG Section 6.2a(iii); Appendix 8.

### Q26
**Topic:** NSAID dose caps (long clinical stem)

A 67-year-old man with hypertension (controlled) and knee OA takes meloxicam 15 mg daily and, for breakthrough pain, adds diclofenac 50 mg twice daily. His GP reviews his pain medicines.

Adapted from CPG Appendix 8 (selected NSAIDs):

| Drug | Dose | Maximum |
|---|---|---|
| Ibuprofen | 400-800 mg 6-8 hourly | 3200 mg/day |
| Diclofenac | 50-150 mg daily in divided doses | 150 mg/day |
| Meloxicam | 7.5-15 mg daily | 15 mg/day (selective for COX-2 only at 7.5 mg) |
| Celecoxib | 200 mg daily | 200 mg/day (OA) |
| Etoricoxib | 60 mg daily | 90 mg/day |

**What is the key problem with his regimen?**

A. Meloxicam 15 mg is COX-2 selective, so it is safe in hypertension
B. Both are within their maximum doses, so the combination is fine
C. Diclofenac 100 mg daily exceeds its maximum daily dose
D. Two NSAIDs together give no benefit and additive side effects

**Answer:** D

**Explanation:** Combination therapy with more than one NSAID or COX-2 inhibitor should never be used: there is no benefit and side effects are additive. He is on meloxicam plus diclofenac, with hypertension adding risk (NSAIDs need caution in hypertension, CVD, renal or hepatic disease and the elderly). **Reference:** CPG Recommendation 9; Appendix 8.

### Q27
**Topic:** Renal and cardiovascular safety

A 73-year-old man with knee OA, CKD stage 3 and ischaemic heart disease is considering an NSAID for a flare. He is otherwise stable.

**Which advice matches the CPG?**

A. COX-2 inhibitors carry no CV risk and can be used freely
B. Safe in CKD stage 3, so no renal monitoring is needed
C. Naproxen has the highest CV risk, so it should be avoided
D. Lowest effective dose for the shortest time, with regular renal monitoring

**Answer:** D

**Teaching point:** With CKD or CVD: lowest effective dose, shortest duration and regular renal monitoring.

**Reference:** CPG Section 6.2a(iii); Recommendation 9.

### Q32

### Q35
**Topic:** Algorithm sequence (long clinical stem)

A 68-year-old woman with symptomatic knee OA is on education, weight loss, exercise and physiotherapy. Paracetamol with a topical NSAID has failed to control her pain. She has no GI or renal risk factors and no seizures or depression.

**According to the CPG algorithm, what is the next step?**

A. Immediate referral for arthroplasty
B. Oral prednisolone before any other agent
C. Tramadol or a lowest-dose NSAID, with PPI if GI risk is high
D. Intra-articular steroid before any oral analgesic

**Answer:** C

**Teaching point:** OA algorithm: lifestyle → paracetamol ± topical NSAIDs → tramadol/NSAIDs → IA steroid → orthopaedics.

## Section 4: Surgery

### Q36
**Topic:** Arthroscopic lavage

A 60-year-old man with knee OA and persistent pain asks for arthroscopic washout. He has no locking, catching or giving way.

**What is the CPG position?**

A. Not offered, except for locking, catching or giving way
B. Offered only under age 50, whatever the symptoms
C. First-line surgery, as it beats optimised medical therapy
D. Offered to all with persistent pain, to delay replacement

**Answer:** A

**Explanation:** Studies show arthroscopic lavage with or without debridement gives no additional benefit in pain or function compared with optimised physical and medical therapy. The CPG recommends against it (Grade A) except in OA with mechanical symptoms such as locking, catching or giving way, from loose bodies or flaps of meniscus or cartilage.

**Teaching point:** No arthroscopic washout for OA unless there are mechanical symptoms (locking, catching, giving way).

**Reference:** CPG Section 8.1; Recommendation 15.

### Q37
**Topic:** High tibial osteotomy

A 45-year-old active man has isolated medial compartment knee OA with varus alignment and knee flexion of 130 degrees. He wants to avoid early knee replacement.

**Which statement matches the CPG?**

A. Lateral compartment OA in the elderly; later TKR is easier
B. Isolated medial OA, under 50, flexion ≥120°; later TKR is harder
C. Tricompartmental OA over 65; later TKR is easier
D. Any compartment, but flexion must be under 90°

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** HTO realigns the mechanical axis away from the diseased medial compartment, and works best in isolated medial compartment arthritis, particularly in patients under 50 with at least 120 degrees of knee flexion. Disadvantages: asymmetrical appearance and a technically more challenging TKR if needed later.

### Q38
**Topic:** Total joint replacement: indications and contraindications (long clinical stem)

A 52-year-old man with severe knee OA has failed all other therapies. He has night pain, difficulty with self-care and recent deterioration. He has a chronic leg ulcer with cellulitis and unrealistic expectations of returning to marathon running.

**Which statement best describes the CPG's view?**

A. TKR is indicated at diagnosis, with no contraindications
B. Active infection is relative; age <55 is absolute
C. Active infection is absolute; age <55 and poor compliance are relative
D. TKR is contraindicated under age 70; sport is encouraged after

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Total joint replacement is indicated in severe OA that has failed all other therapies, with indications including sleep-disturbing pain, ADL limitation, psychosocial impact and recent deterioration. Active infection is an absolute contraindication to TKR; relative contraindications include young age under 55, poor compliance, regional pain disorders and unrealistic expectations. **Teaching point:** TKR: absolute contraindication is active infection. Relative: age <55, non-compliance, regional pain, unrealistic expectations.

**Reference:** CPG Section 8.3.

B. Superior to TKR in every respect, with 99% survival at 10 years
C. Slower recovery and more blood loss than TKR, with 85% survival
