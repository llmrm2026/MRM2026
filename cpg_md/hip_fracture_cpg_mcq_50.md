# Geriatric Hip Fracture CPG – 50 MCQs

Source: Malaysian CPG Management of Geriatric Hip Fracture (1st Ed., 2023). Hard, MRCP-style, four close options.

### Q1
**Topic:** Occult hip fracture imaging

An 86-year-old woman falls at home and cannot weight-bear. She has a non-MRI-conditional cardiac pacemaker for complete heart block. The left leg is not shortened or rotated, but she cannot perform a straight leg raise and log-roll causes groin pain. AP pelvis (15° internal rotation) and lateral femur radiographs are reported as normal.

**Which is the most appropriate next investigation?**

A. CT scan of the left hip and pelvis
B. Repeat radiographs after 5 to 7 days
C. Technetium bone scan at 72 hours
D. Ultrasound of the left hip joint

**Answer:** A

**Explanation:** A normal radiograph does not exclude a fracture. MRI is the reference test but is contraindicated by the non-conditional pacemaker, so CT is the next test (it detected 39% of occult fractures in a meta-analysis). Delay with repeat films or early bone scan risks missed fracture and a prolonged immobility.

**Teaching point:** Normal X-ray with clinical suspicion = occult fracture until proven otherwise; MRI first, CT if MRI is unavailable within 24 hours or contraindicated.

**Reference:** CPG Section 3 (Diagnosis), Recommendation 1

### Q2
**Topic:** Radiographic views

A 79-year-old man is brought to the Emergency Department after a fall. The right leg is shortened and externally rotated. The radiographer asks which views are required to assess for hip fracture.

**Which combination represents the standard plain radiographic views?**

A. AP pelvis in neutral with a frog-leg lateral
B. Lateral femur only with a Judet oblique view
C. AP pelvis with 15° internal rotation and AP and lateral femur
D. Inlet and outlet pelvic views with a lateral femur

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** The CPG standard series is an AP pelvis with 15° internal rotation (to profile the femoral neck) plus AP and lateral views of the femur. Frog-leg views can displace an unstable fracture; Judet and inlet/outlet views assess the acetabulum and pelvic ring.

**Teaching point:** Internal rotation of 15° brings the femoral neck parallel to the film; avoid frog-leg lateral in suspected fracture.

**Reference:** CPG Section 3 (Diagnosis)

### Q3
**Topic:** Pre-operative analgesia

An 91-year-old woman with moderate dementia, CrCl 28 mL/min and a displaced neck of femur fracture has a pain score of 4/10 at rest and 8/10 on movement. She has had no analgesia. She is awaiting surgery within 24 hours.

**Which is the most appropriate initial analgesic strategy?**

A. Regular intravenous morphine infusion with antiemetic cover
B. Regular paracetamol, with titrated low-dose opioid for breakthrough pain
C. Regular celecoxib 200 mg twice daily with a proton pump inhibitor
D. Regular tramadol 100 mg every six hours with a laxative

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Paracetamol is first line, with a low-dose opioid (e.g. morphine 2-3 mg SC/PO) for breakthrough pain. Celecoxib is poorly suited to a CrCl of 28; tramadol needs dose reduction at CrCl <30 and carries confusion risk; a morphine infusion risks delirium and respiratory depression in an opioid-naive frail patient.

**Teaching point:** Start low, one agent at a time; paracetamol baseline, opioid for breakthrough.

**Reference:** CPG Section 4, Recommendation 2; Appendix 3

### Q4
**Topic:** Nerve block

A 88-year-old man on dual antiplatelet therapy after a recent stent has a displaced intracapsular fracture. Despite regular paracetamol and morphine 2.5 mg subcutaneously, he scores 8/10 on movement and becomes drowsy and confused after the second opioid dose.

**Which is the most appropriate next step to control pain?**

A. Increase the morphine dose to 5 mg every 4 hours
B. Add oral dihydrocodeine 60 mg every six hours
C. Apply skin traction to the injured limb
D. Fascia iliaca or femoral nerve block

**Answer:** D

**Explanation:** The CPG advises a femoral nerve or fascia iliaca block when pain persists despite analgesia, sparing opioids and reducing delirium. More opioid or another opioid-type drug worsens drowsiness; routine skin traction does not improve pain.

**Teaching point:** Regional blocks are opioid-sparing and preferred when opioids cause sedation or confusion.

**Reference:** CPG Section 4, Recommendations 2 and 3

### Q5
**Topic:** Skin traction

A junior doctor asks whether to apply skin traction to a 76-year-old man with a stable intertrochanteric fracture awaiting theatre for 36 hours.

**Which statement about pre-operative skin traction is most accurate?**

A. It reduces pain scores and should be applied for all patients
B. It improves fracture reduction and eases later fixation
C. It lowers pressure sore incidence in prolonged waiting
D. It is not recommended routinely as it gives no analgesic benefit

**Answer:** D

**Explanation:** The CPG recommends against routine pre-operative skin traction; it does not reduce pain or ease surgery and can cause skin problems.

**Teaching point:** Do not use routine skin traction in hip fracture; use analgesia/nerve blocks instead.

**Reference:** CPG Section 4, Recommendation 3

### Q6
**Topic:** COX-2 inhibitors

A 83-year-old woman with ischaemic heart disease on aspirin 75 mg, eGFR 52 mL/min/1.73 m² and previous dyspepsia has a hip fracture. The team considers adding a selective COX-2 inhibitor to paracetamol.

**Which is the most appropriate statement about this plan?**

A. Etoricoxib 120 mg daily is safe in view of her gastric history
B. Short-course COX-2 inhibitor with a proton pump inhibitor, used with caution
C. COX-2 inhibitors are first-line for all pre-operative hip fracture pain
D. Celecoxib 400 mg twice daily gives superior analgesia without risk

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** COX-2 inhibitors may be used with caution; with aspirin and prior GI symptoms a PPI should be added, the lowest dose for the shortest time used, and renal and cardiovascular risk weighed. Higher doses increase GI and cardiovascular events.

**Teaching point:** COX-2 inhibitors: low dose, short course, PPI cover, caution in IHD and renal impairment.

**Reference:** CPG Section 4, Appendix 3

### Q7
**Topic:** Tramadol interactions

A 80-year-old woman with depression on sertraline 100 mg daily fractures her hip. The ward prescribes tramadol 50 mg every 6 hours. Her creatinine clearance is 24 mL/min. Twelve hours later she is agitated and tremulous, with myoclonus and a temperature of 38.2°C.

**Which is the most likely explanation?**

A. Opioid withdrawal syndrome
B. Hypoactive delirium from sepsis
C. Serotonin syndrome from tramadol with an SSRI
D. Neuroleptic malignant syndrome

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Tramadol with an SSRI can precipitate serotonin syndrome (agitation, tremor, myoclonus, hyperthermia), and renal impairment (CrCl <30) increases accumulation. NMS requires an antipsychotic and rigidity; withdrawal does not cause myoclonus with fever in this context.

**Teaching point:** Avoid tramadol with SSRIs; reduce dose in CrCl <30 mL/min.

**Reference:** Appendix 3 (Pre-operative analgesia in GHF)

### Q8
**Topic:** Paracetamol dosing

A 78-year-old man with alcohol-related liver disease (Child-Pugh B) and 46 kg body weight sustains a hip fracture. He requires regular paracetamol.

**What is the most appropriate maximum daily dose?**

A. A reduced maximum, about 1 to 2 g daily
B. The standard 4 g daily with daily LFTs
C. 3 g daily, split into three doses only
D. Paracetamol should be avoided completely

**Answer:** A

**Explanation:** The CPG advises reducing the maximum dose by 50-75% in hepatic insufficiency or alcohol abuse (i.e. about 1-2 g daily). It is not contraindicated; the standard dose is unsafe.

**Teaching point:** Reduce paracetamol maximum dose 50-75% in hepatic insufficiency/alcohol misuse.

**Reference:** Appendix 3

### Q9
**Topic:** Delirium screening

An 84-year-old resident of a nursing home with mild cognitive impairment is admitted with a hip fracture. On day 2 she is drowsy, inattentive and cannot state the months backward. Her 4AT is scored.

**Which finding contributes most directly to the 4AT assessment?**

A. Acute change or fluctuating course in mental status
B. Presence of a urinary catheter on admission
C. Length of time since the last bowel motion
D. Pre-operative haemoglobin of 9.8 g/dL

**Answer:** A

**Explanation:** 4AT tests alertness, AMT4 orientation, attention (months backward) and acute change or fluctuating course. Catheter, bowels and haemoglobin are delirium risk contributors but not 4AT items.

**Teaching point:** 4AT = Alertness, AMT4, Attention, Acute change/fluctuation.

**Reference:** CPG Section 5 (Recommendation 5); Appendix 4

### Q10

Four patients are admitted on the same day with hip fractures: a 72-year-old independent man with normal cognition on paracetamol; an 84-year-old woman from a nursing home with pre-existing cognitive impairment, a urinary tract infection and morphine prescribed; a 76-year-old woman with osteoporosis and a normal sodium; and a 69-year-old man with normal vision due to receive a spinal block.

**Which patient has the highest risk of post-operative delirium?**

A. The 72-year-old independent man
B. The 76-year-old woman with osteoporosis
C. The 84-year-old institutionalised woman on morphine

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** The CPG lists age >80, institutional residence, morphine use and infection as delirium risk factors, in addition to baseline cognitive impairment.

**Teaching point:** Age >80, institution, opioids, infection and dementia are the key delirium risks.

### Q11
**Topic:** CGA domains

A geriatrician reviews a 90-year-old man pre-operatively. He lives alone, uses a stick, takes eight medications and has a Clinical Frailty Scale of 6. He has lost weight over six months. The registrar plans a comprehensive geriatric assessment.

**Which pairing of CGA dimension and tool is correct?**

A. Psychological – Barthel Index

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** MNA-SF belongs to the physical dimension (nutritional status). Barthel is functional, GDS and 4AT are psychological.

**Teaching point:** CGA spans physical, functional, psychological and social domains.

**Reference:** Appendix 4

### Q13
**Topic:** Transfusion threshold

A 82-year-old woman with a displaced intracapsular fracture has a haemoglobin of 9.2 g/dL. She is asymptomatic, haemodynamically stable and has no ischaemic heart disease. Cross-matched blood is available.

**Which is the most appropriate approach?**

A. Postpone surgery until haemoglobin exceeds 12 g/dL
B. Transfuse two units pre-operatively regardless of symptoms
C. Give intravenous iron and delay surgery by one week
D. Proceed to surgery with blood available, transfusing if symptomatic

**Answer:** D

**Explanation:** Haemoglobin 8-10 g/dL in an asymptomatic patient without IHD, with blood available, does not need to delay surgery. Ideal Hb is ≥10 g/dL, but delay raises mortality and complications.

**Teaching point:** Do not delay hip fracture surgery for Hb 8-10 g/dL in a stable patient without IHD.

**Reference:** CPG Section 5

### Q15
**Topic:** Neuraxial block after clopidogrel

A 81-year-old man with a coronary stent takes aspirin 75 mg and clopidogrel 75 mg daily. He presents with an intertrochanteric fracture. The anaesthetist favours a spinal anaesthetic.

**Which is the most appropriate plan?**

A. Delay surgery 5 to 7 days to allow a safe spinal
B. Give platelet transfusion before performing the spinal

**Reference:** CPG Section 7, Recommendation 7; Appendix 5

### Q30
**Topic:** Epidemiology and mortality

A 74-year-old Malaysian woman with osteoporosis asks her family physician about her risk after her 81-year-old mother sustained a hip fracture. She asks how common hip fracture is locally, who is most affected, and what her mother's outlook is.

**Which statement best reflects the local data quoted in the CPG?**

A. Incidence is about 90 per 100,000 aged 50 and above, peaking at 70-79 years, with women affected about twice as often as men
B. Incidence is about 90 per 100,000 aged 50 and above, peaking at 80-89 years, with men affected about twice as often as women
C. Incidence is about 90 per 10,000 aged 50 and above, peaking at 70-79 years, with equal rates in both sexes
D. Incidence is about 9 per 100,000 aged 50 and above, peaking at 60-69 years, with women affected about twice as often as men

**Answer:** A

**Explanation:** The CPG quotes about 90 per 100,000 in those aged 50 and over, a peak at 70-79 years and a female to male ratio of about 2:1. One-year mortality is about 30%. The distractors alter one parameter each (age peak, sex ratio, denominator).

**Teaching point:** Malaysian hip fracture incidence 90/100,000 (≥50), peak 70-79 years, women 2:1, one-year mortality about 30%.

**Reference:** CPG Section 1 (Introduction)

### Q31
**Topic:** Risk factors for hip fracture

A 71-year-old woman with rheumatoid arthritis attends clinic. She smokes, drinks alcohol most days, has taken prednisolone 7.5 mg daily for 4 years, omeprazole 40 mg daily for reflux and sertraline for depression, and has a T-score of -2.7 at the femoral neck. D. Regular calcium with vitamin D supplementation

**Answer:** D

### Q32
**Topic:** Opioid choice in renal impairment

An 88-year-old woman has a displaced intracapsular fracture. She has CKD stage 4 (creatinine clearance 22 mL/min), a mild cognitive impairment and takes sertraline. Paracetamol 500 mg six-hourly is insufficient, and a nerve block has been performed with good effect on movement. She still needs a regular oral agent for background pain overnight.

**Which analgesic is the most appropriate addition?**

A. Oxycodone 2.5 mg orally, 6-hourly, titrated cautiously
B. Tramadol 50 mg orally every 6 hours, titrated upwards
C. Dihydrocodeine 60 mg orally every 6 hours, titrated upwards
D. Etoricoxib 90 mg orally once daily for five days

**Answer:** A

**Explanation:** Tramadol interacts with sertraline (serotonin syndrome) and needs dose reduction at CrCl <30 mL/min. Dihydrocodeine at 60 mg is a large dose for frail renal impairment and accumulates; etoricoxib is hazardous in CKD 4. Low-dose oxycodone titrated carefully is a pragmatic option.

**Teaching point:** In renal impairment avoid COX-2 inhibitors; reduce tramadol doses at CrCl <30 and avoid with SSRIs.

**Reference:** CPG Appendix 3

### Q34

### Q35
**Topic:** Falls prevention algorithm

A 76-year-old man attends a routine health visit. He had one fall at home 6 months ago with no injury, feels unsteady when walking on uneven ground, and has a gait speed of 0.7 m/s. There was no loss of consciousness, no lying on the floor and he is not frail.

**According to the CPG algorithm, what is the appropriate risk category and intervention?**

A. Low risk; education on falls prevention and advice on physical activity
B. Intermediate risk; tailored exercises for balance, gait and strength, with physiotherapy referral
C. High risk; multifactorial falls risk assessment with 30 to 90 days follow-up
D. Low risk; reassess in one year with no specific intervention

**Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Positive 3KQ (fall in past year, unsteady) triggers assessment of fall severity; there is no severity feature, but gait is impaired (speed ≤0.8 m/s), placing him in the intermediate group: secondary prevention with tailored exercise for balance, gait and strength.

**Teaching point:** Impaired gait with no severity features = intermediate risk.

**Reference:** CPG Algorithm on risk stratification, assessment and interventions for community-dwelling older adults

### Q36

### Q50
**Topic:** Implant selection in intertrochanteric fracture

A 74-year-old man has a 31A2 intertrochanteric fracture. The radiograph shows a thin lateral wall, a comminuted posteromedial fragment and a fracture line extending below the lesser trochanter.

**Which feature most strongly favours a cephalomedullary nail over a dynamic hip screw?**

A. The fracture is on the dominant side
B. The posteromedial fragment is large and undisplaced
C. Lateral wall thickness below 20.5 mm
D. He walked without aids before the fall

**Answer:** C

**Explanation:** A lateral wall thickness <20.5 mm (with reverse oblique pattern, posteromedial buttress loss or subtrochanteric extension) predicts instability and favours CMN. Dominant side and pre-injury mobility do not determine implant choice.

**Teaching point:** Unstable pattern (thin lateral wall) → CMN.

**Reference:** CPG Section 8, Recommendation 10
